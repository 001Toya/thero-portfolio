MY JOURNEY

Two paths. One constant: creating.

My journey has never been about having everything figured out from the beginning. Both software engineering and art became meaningful to me through curiosity, practice, and the desire to turn ideas into something real.

Software Engineering Journey

From choosing a course to finding a direction

I did not initially choose Software Engineering because I had already decided that it was the path for me. I chose it because it was the closest university programme to what I wanted to study. That changed as I began my studies at Wigwe University.

As I learned programming, I became increasingly interested in the idea of turning manual processes into streamlined, scalable solutions. Programming stopped feeling like just another academic subject and started becoming a way of thinking about problems differently.

Projects such as Order Up and a simple calculator project helped broaden my understanding of what I could build with code and, more importantly, expanded my interest in software development. In the Order Up group project, I served as team leader, helped move the development process forward, and contributed to the manager dashboard.

Stepping into the real world

I am currently gaining professional experience as a Software Engineering Intern at Cubbes, where I have been exposed to how real software teams collaborate, build, test, improve, and document products. My experience so far has introduced me to practical areas such as:

  • Git and GitHub
  • Structured data and SEO
  • Product testing and QA
  • Web development
  • JavaScript and continued development

These experiences have helped me move beyond simply writing code and start understanding the wider process of building software responsibly.

Where I am heading

I am still discovering which area of software engineering I want to pursue most deeply, and I see that as part of the journey rather than something I need to decide immediately. One area that continues to interest me is artificial intelligence — particularly how AI can be used efficiently within my workflow, to solve problems, improve processes, and help build useful systems.

I am still learning, but each project, technical challenge, and professional experience is helping me understand the kind of engineer I want to become.

Artistic Journey

It started with superheroes

My artistic journey began when I was 12 years old in 2019, when I would copy almost every superhero I could find. When I entered secondary school in late 2021, my classmates discovered that I could draw and started asking me to draw them. At the time, my work was far from where it is today, but their encouragement kept me drawing. Eventually, I realized that enthusiasm alone would not take me very far. I wanted to improve.

Learning through curiosity

I turned to YouTube and began teaching myself how to draw, starting with faces and shading. Looking back, I probably started in the wrong order — I was fascinated by shading before I fully understood structure, anatomy, or perspective. But I loved one thing about portraiture: the challenge of taking a real face and trying to reproduce it as accurately as possible with nothing more than paper and pencil.

As I continued learning, I expanded into structure, anatomy, perspective, and form. Learning those fundamentals eventually made my portraits better and helped me understand why the things I had been drawing worked.

From practice to portraiture

Over the years, I kept drawing faces consistently. The progress was gradual, but noticeable. The same people who encouraged me when I was starting could eventually see how far the work had come — and so could I. My mum and elder brother have also been important sources of encouragement throughout that journey.

Today, I mainly identify as a portrait artist, while continuing to explore the possibilities of coloured artwork, digital art, and animation.

Still becoming

Both software engineering and art have taught me something similar: skill grows through curiosity, practice, and the willingness to keep learning. I am still figuring out where both paths will take me, but I know I want to keep building, creating, and improving along the way.
